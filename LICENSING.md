Most people need nothing more than the AGPL. Running Copse, including at work,
and changing it for yourself are covered.

## When to ask for a commercial licence

Consider a commercial licence if you want to:

- ship a modified Copse, or a product built on Copse's code, without publishing
  your source;
- offer a modified Copse to users over a network without publishing your source;
- distribute Copse through a channel whose terms conflict with the AGPL, such as
  some app stores;
- embed Copse's packages in a proprietary product.

Commercial terms are agreed case by case. Email
[licensing@copse.dev](mailto:licensing@copse.dev) with what you plan to build
and how it will be distributed.

## What a commercial licence does not cover

- **Third-party components.** Copse bundles components under their own licences,
  listed in [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). A commercial
  licence from the Copse copyright holder cannot change those terms, and their
  obligations still apply.
- **Earlier releases.** Releases up to and including `0.1.0-beta.8` were
  published under the Apache License 2.0, and that grant remains in force for
  those versions.

## Why contributions need a CLA

Offering Copse under a second licence is only possible if the copyright holder
has the right to license every part of it. Outside contributors keep the
copyright in their work, so each signs the [Copse Contributor Licence
Agreement](CLA.md) once. It grants a licence broad enough to include
contributions in commercially licensed builds, and in return commits that every
accepted contribution stays available under the AGPL or another open source
licence. [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md#licensing-your-contribution)
describes the process.
